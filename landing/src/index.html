Features

Made for the clinic, not the cloud

What is built today, and what is coming, labelled honestly.

Built

Works offline

The phone's own database is the register. The cloud is a copy that fills in when there is signal.

Built

Quick register

Villages and reasons you use most appear as buttons. A possible duplicate is flagged before it is saved.

Built

Patient card with QR

Print a bank-card size card. The QR holds only the card number, so a lost card tells a stranger nothing.

Built

Monthly LGA summary

Visits by age and sex, maternal and child care, and vaccine doses, counted from your own records.

Built

Duplicate finder

Finds the same patient registered twice, even with spelling differences, and merges the records safely.

Built

PIN lock and audit log

A PIN locks the phone. Every change is recorded, without patient names, so the facility can see who did what.

Built

Encrypted backup

One password-protected file with the whole register, that the clinic keeps and controls.

Built

Low Battery Mode

When the battery runs low, Prima pauses syncing and saves power. Records are still saved at once.

Built

FHIR and DHIS2 files

Standard files for health information systems. You decide what is shared, and nothing is sent automatically.

Built

Installs from the browser

Add it to the home screen of an Android phone. No app store, and a small download.

Coming

Igbo, Hausa and Yoruba

Each translation will be checked by a native speaker before it goes live.

Coming

Sync over clinic Wi-Fi

For clinics with power and a router but no internet: tablets sharing records with each other.

Coming

SMS reminders

Reminders for visits and vaccines, only to people who agreed to receive them.

What we heard

Questions people asked us

Prima is being built in public, and the comments shape it.

"Can CHEWs really use it?"

That is why registering takes one screen, buttons are big enough for gloves, and we test with real nurses and CHEWs before launch.

"What about days with no internet at all?"

Everything already works offline. Tablet-to-tablet sync over the clinic's Wi-Fi is being built for the clinic with power but no data.

"Can it speak our languages?"

Igbo, Hausa and Yoruba are planned. Each will be reviewed by a native speaker, because a wrong word on a clinical screen is a safety problem.

Careful with patient records

Privacy first, and honest about what is left to do

Records stay on the clinic's phone first

The register lives on the device. The cloud copy keeps every clinic's records walled off from every other clinic's.

Locks, roles and a change log

A PIN on every phone, admin-only actions for sensitive steps, and an audit log that the app can add to but never edit. It records what happened without recording who the patients are.

Before any real patient data: Prima goes through a data protection review and a clinical review. Until then it is tested with made-up patients only. We do not claim certification we do not have.

The story

Why I am building Prima

Hi, I'm Chukwunonso Ofili. I'm a public health student and a self-taught developer in Enugu, Nigeria.

Many health tools assume steady internet. Many primary health centres do not have it. In many clinics the register is still on paper, and the monthly report is counted by hand at the end of the month.

So I asked a simple question: what would a patient register look like if it was built for the clinic with power on some days and network on fewer? Prima is my answer so far. I speak Igbo as my first language, so I also know how much a tool in English alone leaves out.

I'm building it in public, one day at a time. The comments on my posts are where people tell me what is wrong with it, and I fix those things first.

Chukwunonso Ofili, Enugu, Nigeria

Questions

Straight answers

Does it really work with no internet?
Yes. You need internet once to install it and sign in. After that, registering patients, recording visits and printing cards all work offline. Records upload by themselves when the connection returns.
What if the phone is lost?
The phone locks with a PIN, and the clinic can make an encrypted backup file. Once records have synced, they are also in the clinic's cloud copy and can be loaded on another phone.
Where are the records kept, and who can see them?
On the clinic's phones first, and in a cloud copy where each clinic's records are walled off from every other clinic's. Only that clinic's signed-in staff can open them in the app. Prima does not sell or share patient records, and a full privacy policy will be published before the pilot.
Is Prima approved or certified?
Not yet. It is a pilot. Before it holds any real patient data it goes through a data protection review and a clinical review, and it is tested with made-up patients until then.
What does it cost?
Free for small clinics (up to 200 patients). The planned paid plans are ₦2,500 a month for Basic and ₦5,000 a month for Pro. The first 5 pilot clinics get Pro free for 3 months.
Which phones does it work on?
Android phones with the Chrome browser are the focus, and it also runs on laptops. iPhones can open it, but offline behaviour there has not been tested yet.
Can it send our monthly numbers to DHIS2?
Prima prepares a DHIS2 data file on the Pro plan. A person imports it, so nothing leaves the clinic without a decision.
Will it be in Igbo, Hausa or Yoruba?
That is planned, and each language will be checked by a native speaker before release.
